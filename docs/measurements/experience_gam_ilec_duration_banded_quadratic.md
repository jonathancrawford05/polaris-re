## Caveats

- 277,173 cells with uw_class == 'U' (underwriting class *unknown*, not *not applicable*) were held out of this class-conditioned fit. 'NA' rows are retained as their own stratum. Pass keep_unknown_uw_class=True to include them.
- 61 base strata (0.009% of exposure) had zero deaths across the whole window and were dropped — their base rate is not estimable and they carry no trend information.
- 152 cells had zero exposure and were dropped — a Poisson offset needs exposure * q_base > 0, and a cell nobody was exposed in carries no information. They held 0.0 deaths, as expected.
- The A/E section covers the full loaded book (126,223 cells), while the fitted surface covers 125,676 — the zero-death strata above are excluded from the fit but retained in A/E, because SOA's denominator covers every cell they priced. The two populations differ by 0.009% of exposure.
- Duration is controlled for via 9 bands (start policy years [1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 11, 16, 21, 26]), each collapsed onto a fixed representative duration so the value is calendar-invariant and cannot reintroduce the mix confound it exists to remove. Selection *within* a band is still pooled — the first three years are banded singly for that reason.
- Pearson dispersion φ = 1.16, so the quasi-Poisson covariance scaling was applied: every band below is 1.08x wider than a plain-Poisson fit would have reported. The point estimates are unchanged — scaling touches the covariance only. A φ this far above 1 means unmodelled heterogeneity, which is expected on real grouped data and is why the scaling is not optional here.
- The base offset is estimated from these same cells, so `overall_ae` is ~1 by construction and is not an independent check on the level. The fitted improvement is unaffected: it is a calendar contrast, and every calendar-invariant term — the offset included — cancels in the difference.
